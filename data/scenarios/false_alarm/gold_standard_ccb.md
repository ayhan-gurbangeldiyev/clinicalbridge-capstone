# Gold Standard CCB — Scenario 2: False Alarm

**Patient:** PT-005, James Whitfield, 71M  
**Alert:** Glucose 214 mg/dL — URGENT  
**Scenario:** Glucose spike is explained by vacation dietary indiscretion on a cruise. Patient is adherent to medications, spike is transient and already resolving.

---

## 1. Alert Summary
- **Triggered by:** Glucose 214 mg/dL (threshold upper limit 180 mg/dL; +34 above threshold)
- **Urgency:** ROUTINE (downgraded from alert's initial URGENT classification after contextual review)
- **Trend:** Glucose elevated days 20-23 only; baseline returns to 140-150 range. Single-cluster spike, not a sustained pattern.

## 2. Patient Snapshot
- **Active conditions:** Type 2 diabetes mellitus (E11.9)
- **Current medications:** Metformin 1000mg BID, Glipizide 5mg QD
- **Key history:** Well-controlled T2DM, HbA1c 7.1 at last check (2025-10-15), retired teacher, walks daily

## 3. Contextual Analysis
The glucose elevation occurred exclusively during days 20-23 of the monitoring period, aligning precisely with a cruise vacation documented in the anamnesis. The patient explicitly notes increased carbohydrate consumption from buffet food and difficulty controlling dietary intake during travel. Medication adherence is confirmed as 95% with no missed doses during this period. The glucose value of 214 mg/dL, while above threshold, is a modest elevation in a patient whose fasting glucose typically runs 140-150 on a managed diet. Vitals (BP, HR, SpO2, weight) show no concurrent deterioration. By day 23, glucose was already trending back toward baseline (145 mg/dL), consistent with dietary normalization. This pattern is a benign dietary excursion, not a treatment failure.

## 4. Risk Assessment
- **Primary risk:** Low — transient dietary-induced hyperglycemia in a medication-adherent patient; no ketosis risk (Type 2, not Type 1); no signs of infection or metabolic stress
- **Differential:** Dietary excursion (most likely, confidence 0.93) vs. medication interaction vs. new illness (no supporting evidence)
- **Confidence:** 0.93 — Timeline correlation between dietary change and glucose spike is unambiguous

## 5. Recommended Actions
1. **No immediate intervention required** — Glucose already normalizing; continue current regimen. Evidence: RPM trend (glucose trending down day 23), anamnesis (diet explanation)
2. **Educate patient on travel glucose management** — Discuss pre-meal bolus strategies or temporary dose adjustment for high-carbohydrate periods during future travel. Evidence: anamnesis (patient question about medication adjustment during travel)
3. **Confirm HbA1c at next scheduled visit** — If vacation excursions are frequent, cumulative impact on HbA1c should be assessed. Evidence: EHR lab history

## 6. Uncertainties and Gaps
- **LOW_CONFIDENCE:** Cannot independently verify dietary history — based solely on patient self-report in anamnesis
- **MISSING_DATA:** No post-vacation glucose readings after day 23 to confirm full return to baseline

## 7. Immediate Review Required
**No** — Glucose trending down, no end-organ symptoms, medication adherent. Routine follow-up at next scheduled appointment is appropriate.
